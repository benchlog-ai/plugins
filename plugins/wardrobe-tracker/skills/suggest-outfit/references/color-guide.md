## Pairing principles to apply when suggesting or judging combinations

- Neutrals (navy, grey, charcoal, white, beige, camel, brown, black) anchor an outfit; aim for one or two non-neutral accents at most.
- Tonal: shades of one hue (light blue shirt + navy blazer) read polished and suit the office.
- Complementary pairs with muted saturation work well: navy + camel/rust, olive + burgundy, teal + rust.
- Match leathers: belt matches shoes (brown with brown, black with black); watch strap may follow them.
- Formality should be within one step across all pieces (formality field 1–5).
- Season: linen, light colors for summer; wool, flannel, deeper tones for autumn/winter.
- Respect the user's own rated `combos`: anything marked `avoid` is never suggested; `works` combos are favored.

## The user's own colouring and preferences

Read `profile/me` (see `data-model.md`) when choosing colors near the face (shirts, tops, kurtas, scarves, ties). Use only what the user has told you; never guess their colouring from a photo or a name.

- `flattering_colors`: prefer these for tops and statement pieces.
- `colors_to_avoid`: keep these away from the face; use them only lower down or in small accents, and never when the user says they dislike them outright.
- `undertone`, if given: warm undertones usually suit camel, rust, olive, mustard, cream and warm browns; cool undertones usually suit navy, royal blue, burgundy, grey, pure white and lavender; neutral suits most. Treat this as a tie-breaker, never an override of what the user says they like.
- If the profile has none of these, skip this section and rely on the pairing principles and the user's `combos`.
